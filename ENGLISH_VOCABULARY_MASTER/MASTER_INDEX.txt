1. WHAT THIS SYSTEM IS
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A network-based vocabulary curriculum for an Azerbaijani-speaking learner. Words are never taught
   alone: every MAIN WORD is the hub of a small network made of its word family, 7–8 natural
   expressions, example sentences and a usage explanation. A global database guarantees that no word
   is ever taught twice anywhere between A1 and C2. Fixed phrases (get up, at least) are taught in
   fixed-phrase lessons, built only from words that were taught earlier.
   This is a finite, growing database. It does not contain every English word, and CEFR levels are
   not universally fixed lists. Each lesson was placed at a level by the author, according to the
   difficulty of the expression network it teaches, and the placement was then cross-checked against
   two open CEFR word lists (CEFR-J for A1–B2, Octanove for C1–C2). Where those lists give a
   headword a different level, the lesson says so in a "Level note"; the full comparison is in
   database/qa_report.txt.

4. THE GLOBAL NO-REPETITION SYSTEM (exactly as enforced by the checker)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   R1. One database for all levels. It is never reset between A1 and C2.
   R2. Every main word, every word-family form and every content word inside an expression is
       registered the first time it is taught. Registering it a second time is an error and the
       build stops.
   R3. Inside an expression, each word must be (a) the main word of that lesson or one of its listed
       family forms, (b) a structural word from A1 Section 0, or (c) a brand-new word that has never
       been taught. A word that was taught in any other lesson may not appear in an expression.
   R4. Matching is done on dictionary forms, not on spelling: result/results, make/made/making,
       child/children, shop/shopping are the same item. Genuinely different words that merely look
       alike (e.g. new/news) must be declared one by one in source/distinct_lexemes.txt and are
       listed in Record 6.
   R5. Every expression is unique in the whole system.
   R6. A hyphenated compound (long-term, old-fashioned) is one dictionary item of its own.
   R7. Example sentences and usage explanations may recycle words that were already taught, and they
       are where very common collocations of two already-taught words are shown. They never
       introduce new teaching targets.
   R8. Main words planned for future parts (the roadmap in section 11) are reserved: they cannot be
       used up as collocates in other lessons before their own lesson is written.
   R9. Words from the same family that are taught in different lessons (each form still only once)
       are cross-referenced, so the network stays connected without repetition (Record 6b).
   R10. A fixed phrase (get up, at least, in fact, look forward to sth) is an item of its own. It is
       taught once, in a fixed-phrase lesson; every word inside it must have been taught before that
       lesson, and the same phrase may not appear in another phrase lesson or as an expression of a
       main-word lesson. A phrase lesson never introduces a new word.

12. HOW TO CONTINUE IN A NEW SESSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   1. Keep the whole repository (source/, tools/, database/, ENGLISH_VOCABULARY_MASTER/).
   2. Draft new lessons and test them first:  python3 tools/vocab.py check plan.txt
   3. Add the finished lessons to source/<LEVEL>/ (same line format as the existing files).
   4. Rebuild:  python3 tools/vocab.py build   — the build refuses to write files if any word or
      expression is repeated, so the database can never silently lose or duplicate a record.
   5. When a level grows beyond 80 lessons the builder automatically splits it into numbered parts
      (A1_VOCABULARY_PART_02.txt ...) and lists them in this index.
