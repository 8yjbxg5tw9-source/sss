@ FALLACY | noun | ˈfæləsi | yanlış təsəvvür; məntiqi səhv
forms: fallacy – fallacies
adj: fallacious = yanlış, əsassız
- the fallacy of = ... haqqında yanlış təsəvvür :: The usual pattern: the fallacy OF + noun / -ing
- the gambler's fallacy = qumarbaz yanılması :: gambler = a person who risks money on games of chance
- the sunk-cost fallacy = batmış xərclər yanılması :: sunk-cost = money already spent that cannot be recovered
- the straw-man fallacy = "saman müqəvva" yanılması (rəqibin fikrini təhrif edib təkzib etmək) :: straw-man = a weak, invented version of an opponent's argument
- refute a fallacy = yanlış fikri təkzib etmək :: refute = prove that a statement is wrong
- debunk a fallacy = yanlış fikri ifşa etmək :: debunk = show that an idea or belief is false
- an enduring fallacy = uzunömürlü yanlış təsəvvür :: enduring = lasting for a long time
- a fallacious inference = yanlış nəticə (çıxarış) :: inference = a conclusion reached from the facts available
> It is a fallacy that money always brings happiness. = Pulun həmişə xoşbəxtlik gətirməsi yanlış təsəvvürdür.
> Continuing a bad project because of the money already spent is the sunk-cost fallacy. = Artıq xərclənmiş pula görə uğursuz layihəni davam etdirmək batmış xərclər yanılmasıdır.
> The book debunks several popular fallacies about diet. = Kitab pəhrizlə bağlı bir neçə geniş yayılmış yanlış fikri ifşa edir.
> His conclusion rests on a fallacious inference. = Onun gəldiyi nəticə yanlış çıxarışa əsaslanır.
! A FALLACY is a false idea that many people believe, or an error in reasoning that makes an argument invalid. Naming fallacies is useful in critical essays: the STRAW-MAN fallacy (attacking a distorted version of the other side's view), the FALSE DICHOTOMY, the SLIPPERY SLOPE. FALLACIOUS is the formal adjective. Compare MISCONCEPTION (a wrong idea based on poor understanding) and MYTH (a widely held false belief).

In statistics, a SPURIOUS CORRELATION is a connection between two sets of figures that appears real but is caused by chance or by a third factor. Compare BOGUS (pretending to be real), FAKE (a copy), SPECIOUS (seeming right but actually wrong) and UNFOUNDED (not based on facts).
